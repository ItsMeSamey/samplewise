What do the baselines do?

Balanced counts: start at minimums and add to the smallest affordable sample count. Cost-aware greedy: add the affordable sample with the largest marginal variance reduction per credit. Both respect bounds. Neither is guaranteed optimal with unequal integer costs. Ties follow input order.

03 / KNOW THE LIMITS

A precise answer to a defined problem.

The quantity we minimize

Var(ȳst) = Σ Wh2 Sh2 (1/nh − 1/Nh)

Each population weight is Wh = Nh / ΣN. The finite-population correction makes a fully sampled stratum contribute zero variance. Standard error is the square root of variance, in your outcome's units.

Finite-population S uses denominator N − 1. An estimated within-stratum SD from a pilot is accepted as a planning approximation. A one-member population has SD 0.

What the model assumes

Independent simple random sampling without replacement inside every stratum. Every unit belongs to exactly one stratum. Costs are linear, known and in whole credits.

It does not account for clustering, nonresponse, measurement bias, fixed travel overhead, or uncertainty in population counts. An optimum under these assumptions is not a guarantee of field performance.

Minimums can enforce coverage. With n = 1, planning is possible using an external SD, but variance cannot be estimated from that stratum's sample alone.

What makes it exact

A multiple-choice dynamic program checks feasible integer sample counts at each spend. It retains the best state and reconstructs a global optimum for your input model using Float64 arithmetic.

To keep the browser responsive: at most 12 strata, budget 10,000 credits, population 10 million per stratum, SD up to 1 million, and 20 million estimated DP transitions per solve. Oversized inputs are rejected with guidance.
